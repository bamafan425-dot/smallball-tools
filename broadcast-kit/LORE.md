# SmallBall lore: what the booth knows

Gathered from the Home Plate News archive (all 29 issues, 2002–2009 and the Nov 2026 relaunch) and the SmallBall Library, written up in my own words. The old forum archive (archives.sbforums.org) needs an old forum login to read posts, so it isn't covered yet.

## Timeline
- **1999:** SmallBall Baseball launches (Java client, Real.com / RealArcade). The ball physics, spray tables and pitch generator in today's game are ported straight from it.
- **Feb 18, 2001:** The first season opens. After that a new season started every two weeks for about 18½ years.
- **2002:** The busiest year, with 120,439 sign-ups. HPN reports 65,000+ owners in 30+ countries, and 50–55k games a season.
- **2002:** Changes that year:
  - Pies go from refilling every 12 hours, to every 24 hours, to a two-day pie.
  - The Deluxe Trainer (DT) arrives, along with Free DT Fridays.
  - The Eighth League is added.
  - The assistant password is added.
  - The first new parks are tested: a big-outfield park, then one with a high left-field wall.
  - The 900 Club launches: clubs #900–999 at $65 each.
- **2002:** Two clubs are removed from First League for cheating.
- **2003:** 69,420 clubs are born, the biggest crop ever. Homerun Park opens. Prize tickets for reaching a new ProLeague level begin.
- **Jan 2004:** Letterbox Park is discontinued (it's back now).
- **Fat Tuesday 2004:** The forums go down, and owners write poetry about it.
- **Season 100:** Centennial Park is built as the commemorative stadium.
- **Dec 31, 2006:** Final update to the SmallBall Sports Forums archive.
- **Summer 2019:** Season 483 ends and the lights go out.
- **Nov 20, 2026:** Season 484 opens. 242,191 clubs and 2,906,292 men come back with their old skills.

## People and legends
- **The Commish:** Ed. He still owns the New York 9, club #1. He's on record saying there are no slumps in SmallBall, and his stated goal is to make SmallBall last forever.
- **First League legends:**
  - Ryes Crew: four titles in a row, called "impossible to match."
  - Retry.
  - Dark Elf's Destroyers: for Elf, winning was like getting the morning paper.
  - Minnesota Twins: four titles in their first year.
  - Country Bumpkins: three titles.
  - Neener Beaners: back to back ("Booya!").
  - Legacy of Chaos: GreekPerson, from a Greek village of 2,000, who learned baseball from CNN.
  - Greek Wizard's Montreal Canadiens Hall of Famers.
  - River City Ramblers.
- **Only 38 different First League champions** won between seasons 13 and 82.
- **The original SmallBall Radio (2002):** Synthia Small's season reports, an open phone line, and Johnny Bosco the Frog.

## Slang
- **Zz:** asleep, probably an easy win.
- **Spawners:** owners who make no-name clubs just to beat them.
- **Frogging:** using multiple computers to start multiple games at once.
- **Plug & Play:** bragging on the forums, then challenging everyone.
- **Hit List:** clubs you're coming for.
- **Ghost:** a former owner who still lurks.
- **Green Team:** the forum moderators.
- **Knuckle dusted:** winning First League, then vanishing.
- **U.F.O.:** Unconfirmed Fraudulent Opponent.
- **DT-envy:** wishing you had the Deluxe Trainer.
- **Pulls:** dragging a man across the field to train running.
- **Whole Pie / Focus Training:** spending a whole pie on one skill.
- **Leak:** a bug that gives out extra TP.
- **Test Day:** games are played but don't count.
- **Honor Rating:** a measure of the stars of the clubs you've beaten.
- **4th League:** "a crime against humanity."

## Parks
- **League field:** the default park.
- **Monster:** originally called "Fox Stadium." It rewards speed, gloves and arms.
- **World:** deep fences, for contact and speed clubs.
- **Ocean / Homerun:** pinball-machine scores.
- **BrickYard:** limits right-handed power. A catch off the bricks isn't an out.
- **Alice's:** for lefties. In the poems, the players get distracted flirting with Alice.
- **Field of Dreams:** plays fair. Watch out for the corn.
- **Jurassic:** the cliffs keep everything in the yard. Teams lose their nerve when the dinosaur shows up.
- **Centennial:** the Season 100 park.
- **Letterbox:** wide and short, discontinued in 2004.

## How the game plays (SmallBall Library)
- There's no bullpen: one pitcher per game, and his arm wears down about 1% a pitch.
- An 8-star arm touches about 90 mph; 10 stars reaches 101. In the old days pitchers threw 105–112.
- Low-star clubs make more errors.
- Crowd size depends on both clubs' stars.
- The organ plays the home club's anthem before ProLeague games.
- A season is two weeks long. The ProLeague has nine levels, from Junior up to First, and the top three in First win ticket packs.
- Every man is genetically unique, with his own learn and forget rates.

## The Commish Returns (SmallBall chat, August 16, 2007)
From the full chat log on the ToolKits lore page (docs/lore/commish-returns), saved by people in the room and passed around by email.
- The Commish walked into chat after about two years away. First words: "hell", then "hello". First question: whether his form ID was really 666.
- One owner announced "Hockey comes out tomorrow!" just as he arrived and got a 5-minute ban, missing the moment.
- Q&A moderated by jon2s. "What's next?" "Fix [stuff]." "brb" mid-Q&A (back in ten minutes).
- Chat room record: 39 (old record 29). "EAT IT GRAPEVINE." The night was the 30th anniversary of Elvis's death.
- Why he left: personal; "sb is my baby"; never meant to be away so long. Away years: jobs and projects in the Bay Area, Nokia, an N-Gage pinball game.
- Promises: hockey "so close"; footy and baseball fixed first; new stadiums "maybe one for season 200"; rare parks stay rare; 600 clubs should only be prizes; would donate tickets for prizes.
- A PS2 version of SmallBall was made, but the company funding it went under. He loved the SmallBall Boxing idea.
- Slip was in school and missed it; the saved chat log is how it survived. The Commish signed off with "sniff" and "poof".

## Synthia Small (in the booth)
The original SmallBall Radio (2002) had Synthia Small's season reports. In the revival she's the booth's "O.G.": an older lady who never left the booth, reads season reports from whatever year she thinks it is (2002–2007 details: Eighth League, two-day pies, Free DT Fridays, Ryes Crew on top, the open line to Northern California, Half Moon Bay stickers, Johnny Bosco the frog), and pipes up mid-game. Affectionate, never mean.
